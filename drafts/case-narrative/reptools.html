Giving a vibe-coded catalogue a brand of its own

↗ Download project summary (PDF)

Overview

rep.tools is a live replica-fashion catalogue that grew from about 6,900 items in August to 9,384 by October 2026. It worked, but it had no face of its own. I gave it a brand and a logo, then rebuilt its navigation, catalogue and product pages on them.

Problems

Three problems shaped the redesign.

  1. 1. Forgettable

    A gradient lightning-bolt icon, a gradient headline, glowing orbs and pill badges: bland, and gone from memory a minute later.

  2. 2. Buried

    rep.tools launched as a landing page, and the thing people came for sat one click behind Browse Products.

  3. 3. Missing

    Products had no pages of their own.

Opportunity

How might a working catalogue get a brand of its own and put its products first?

Goals

Each problem became a goal.

  • Forgettable→Distinct

    An identity that speaks its audience’s language.

  • Buried→Upfront

    You land in the catalogue itself.

  • Missing→Built

    Product pages, for the first time.

A brand from the culture

Most visitors arrive from the founder’s TikTok channel (300k+ followers, over 200 million views), already fluent in QC, batches, agents and hauls. So the identity speaks that language: Anton in oversized condensed caps, electric cyan on near-black, Space Mono for anything that behaves like data.

The BATCH logo

One parametric R, the master RETAIL and nine runs, each with one documented tell. It is built into the site’s code and not released yet.

The homepage is now the catalogue

You land in the catalogue itself: category rows you can scroll at once, with filter chips at the top. Almost everyone arrives on a phone, straight from a video, so the rows scroll by thumb and the counted category bar stays visible.

The coupon shows its terms up front

rep.tools earns when a visitor signs up to KakoBuy through its link, so the welcome pack is the one place the site asks for something. The terms are on the card, two plain ways out sit under the button, and on a phone it rises as a bottom sheet.

Results

Every page shares one design system.

The identity lives in one shared stylesheet, and the shop, the tools hub and the buying guide went into the same system.

What I learned

  • AI-generated default styling makes a site forgettable.

    The old site ran fine. It just looked like a hundred others, so nothing about it stayed with a visitor.

  • The homepage should show products right away.

    The founder’s channel sends a steady, already-invested audience straight at it.

  • A brand has to carry the logistics too.

    Going fully hype-drop would have failed the logistics half, where a tracking number or a price has to be legible at a glance.
