Overview

For JAMKADIUM 2026, on the theme CLUTTER, Braxton Hauw and I made a calm fishing puzzle. You throw a magnet, pick apart the junk it pulls up, upgrade your tools and rebuild the town bell across three rivers. It placed 2nd of 20 entries.

Problems

Three problems shaped the game.

  1. 1. Hidden

    An upgrade that only changes a number in a menu is easy to miss.

  2. 2. Overlap

    Our canal overlapped the other rivers on the map.

  3. 3. Distant

    One bell at the end of three rivers was too far away to pull anyone forward.

How might we show progress where the player is already looking?

Goals

Each problem became a goal.

  • Hidden→Visible

    Every upgrade drawn on the tool in the angler's hand.

  • Overlap→Separate

    Each river its own place, opened by a stronger rope.

  • Distant→Close

    A part of the bell in every river.

One clump, one piece at a time

The magnet pulls up one tangled clump of junk. A piece comes free only when nothing above it overlaps it, so every pile is a small puzzle.

Three rivers, three times of day

Rope Strength opens the next river, and each one has its own scene, music and bigger piles.

Upgrades you can see

Five tools, three levels each. The magnet grows and turns gold, and the rope goes from old twine to a golden braid.

The bell, in three parts

One part waits in each river. Rebuild all three and the bell rings again at dawn.

No engine, no audio files.

Plain JavaScript on one canvas. The music, the river ambience and every sound effect are synthesized as you play.

What I learned

  • Upgrades have to be seen.

    Drawing the bigger magnet and the new braid in the angler's hand means every throw shows what the coins bought.

  • Let a fix become a feature.

    The stronger rope started as a fix for the overlapping canal. It gave each river its own scene and became the key between levels.

  • Split the goal so the next step is always close.

    Three parts, one per river, keep the next one in sight.

Claude (Anthropic) adapted my artwork into SVG and generated the game code, item text and documentation. OpenAI Codex helped with testing, bug fixes and code improvements. No image-generation model was used, and the game's credits list every piece.
