Overview

HabitaBull is a lifting app for logging workouts, tracking progress and returning after a break. I researched it in 2023 and designed and built it in 2026, first for the web, now as a native iPhone app. It is still in progress.

Problems

Three problems shaped the app.

  1. 1. Quitting

    71–80% of users abandon their fitness app within 3 months of installing it (Dr. Muscle, StriveCloud, 2024).

  2. 2. Setup

    My first prototype opened with goal-setting and tested badly: people wanted to log a workout first.

  3. 3. Ranking

    A leaderboard ranked by absolute load shames the beginner the whole study was written for.

How might a lifting app keep people coming back after a break?

Goals

Each problem became a goal.

  • Quitting→Returning

    Coming back after a gap counts.

  • Setup→Logging

    The app opens straight to logging a workout.

  • Ranking→Personal

    Progress measured against yourself.

Research outside fitness

I audited 6 fitness apps, studied sobriety apps and ran 6 in-person interviews in Vancouver, with people who formerly used, currently use and never used fitness apps.

Straight to the workout

Today is one button, and the steppers work one-handed between sets, with last time's numbers right there.

No leaderboard

A monthly challenge measured against your own schedule replaced it. A crew sees who trained on which day, with no weights and no ranking.

Comebacks are counted on purpose

Your answer to “Why do you lift?” comes back on the days you do not feel like it. After a gap, the app says the gap does not undo anything.

A bull with an easy smile

The 2026 bull was redrawn with a closed, easy smile, because the app talks to you most on the days you nearly did not come. One colour pass matched every colour in chroma to the brand cyan, with one action colour per screen.

Results

The web version is live.

Every control clears 44 by 44.

Three audits checked touch targets and contrast. One deviation is on purpose: missed days on the consistency grid stay below 3:1, so a skipped day is never as loud as a completed one.

What I learned

  • Sobriety apps showed me how to keep people coming back.

    I Am Sober had already cracked the retention problem I was working on. Three years on, “why do you lift?” is still the second screen.

  • The mascot did real UX work.

    The bull started as decoration and ended up giving the app a voice. He also had to survive 48px, which is how a grin became a smile and a bunny became a bull.

  • Designers should build the thing.

    The research said beginners quit when they feel ranked, and the leaderboard survived that on paper for three years. It did not survive a week of real sessions.
