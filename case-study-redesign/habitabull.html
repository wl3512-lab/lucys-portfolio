Overview

HabitaBull is a lifting app for logging workouts, tracking progress and coming back after a gap. I researched it in 2023 and built a web version in 2026, which is now the design spec for a native iPhone app I am building for the App Store.

Problems

71–80% of users abandon their fitness app within 3 months of installing it (Dr. Muscle, StriveCloud, 2024).

  1. People wanted to log first.

    My first prototype opened with goal-setting and tested badly: people wanted to log a workout first.

  2. Beginners quit when they feel ranked.

    A leaderboard ranked by absolute load shames exactly the beginner the study was written for.

  3. Coming back is where people drop off.

    Most people quit within three months, so the app has to work hardest on the days you nearly do not come.

How might a lifting app keep a beginner coming back, even after a break?

Goals

  • Goals first → Log first

    Open on today’s workout, with last time’s numbers right there.

  • Leaderboard → Your own schedule

    Measure people against their own weeks, not anyone else’s weights.

  • Missed days → Comebacks counted

    After a gap, say plainly that the gap does not undo anything.

Sobriety apps showed me how to keep people coming back.

Studying sobriety apps was an odd move for a fitness project, but I Am Sober had already cracked the retention problem I was working on.

I also audited Strava, NTC, Sworkit, Freeletics, 7min and RunAway, ran 6 in-person interviews in Vancouver with people who formerly used, currently use and never used fitness apps, and tested lo-fi prototypes against four tasks in Figma.

The app opens straight to logging a workout.

Today is one button. The steppers work one-handed between sets, with last time’s numbers right there.

The leaderboard is gone.

A monthly challenge measured against your own schedule replaced it.

The 2023 tutorial came back as a form cue: the bull speaks once, on the set where the mistake usually happens.

Comebacks are counted on purpose.

Your answer to “Why do you lift?” comes back on the days you do not feel like it. After a gap, the app says the gap does not undo anything.

A crew sees who trained on which day, with no weights and no ranking.

The mascot carries the brand.

The 2026 bull was redrawn with a closed, easy smile, because the app talks to you most on the days you nearly did not come.

The Oxide pass matched every colour in chroma to the brand cyan, with one action colour per screen.

What I learned

  1. The leaderboard problem only showed once real people used the app.

    The research said beginners quit when they feel ranked, and the leaderboard survived that finding on paper for three years. It did not survive a week of real sessions. That is the strongest argument I have for designers building the thing.

  2. The mascot did real UX work.

    The bull started as decoration. By the end he gave the app a voice and a reason to celebrate a PR. He also had to survive 48px, which is how a grin became a smile and a bunny became a bull.

How it was made

Audits

Three audits, each recording what was measured and what changed. Every control clears 44 by 44, and contrast was computed for all 21 token pairs in use. One knowing deviation is written down: the consistency grid’s missed cells stay below 3:1 on purpose, because raising them makes every skipped day as loud as every completed one.
