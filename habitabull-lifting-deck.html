2023 / evidence into decisions

Three priorities
from six interviews.

Interviews in Vancouver included current, lapsed and non-users of workout apps. Their different habits shaped the question paths.

Reduce effort
Clutter and slow input interrupted training. Make the next action easy to find.
Allow ownership
People wanted tracking and routines that reflected their needs. Keep plans editable.
Show continuity
Progress and social support mattered. Keep a visible record of effort and return.

This small qualitative study established design priorities; it does not establish retention impact.

Make a plan. Make it personal.

Initial desk research: six fitness apps, I Am Sober and Atomic Habits. These were design references, not evidence that HabitaBull improves retention.

September 2026 / public-documentation review

AI plans are common.
Control still matters.

I reviewed 11 fitness apps and used the comparisons to sharpen three decisions in HabitaBull.

Editable plans
Stance and Fitbod reinforced keeping proposed plans open to adjustment. HabitaBull adds clear save, name and reuse controls.
Fast logging
Hevy and Gravl reinforced the importance of logging. Large controls and last-attempt context keep it central.
A useful return
Consistency support already exists. HabitaBull must make returning easier in the actual flow, not rely on the habit-building label.

Selected examples from the September review. This was not competitor usability testing. Retention impact remains unmeasured.

Start and return / the habit in the interface

Start with a reason.
Return without a reset.

The first visit leads with a quick workout. A saved reason and a distinct welcome-back moment support the next one.

Begin
Start a quick workout before building a full week. Setup can follow the first useful action.
Remember
Show the person’s own reason for lifting, in their own words.
Return
Acknowledge coming back after a gap. Training history remains part of the story.

A shorter-session preview and undo are future concepts; this build resumes the planned session.

Personalization / name, save and reuse

Build it once.
Make it yours.

Adding an ab exercise to leg day should produce a workout you can name, find and use again.

Name
An off-type lift prompts an editable suggestion. Save Legs and abs in Custom workouts.
Reuse
Put the saved workout on another day without rebuilding the exercise list.
Keep in sync
Edits reach linked future days. Completed sessions retain what was actually logged.

Renaming updates its name everywhere it is scheduled.

Progress / attendance, history and photos

Imperfect sessions still count.

The record should reflect real activity, including a short visit, another workout on the same date and progress beyond a load number.

An empty workout start does not count as attendance. Screens use demonstration data and photo stand-ins.

A design tradeoff / community without ranking

A crew without
a leaderboard.

The 2023 concept included a leaderboard. I reconsidered how ranking by load would feel to a beginner.

Tradeoff
Competition appealed to some interviewees. Discouraging new lifters was a concern to test.
Decision
Show who trained, shared photos, likes and replies. Keep monthly targets personal.
Carry-through
A friend’s workout can be copied without copying their lifting numbers.

This is a design judgment, not a demonstrated retention improvement.

HabitaBull · Lucy Liu

AI assistance / less work to set up

Let the app help.
Keep the last word.

Assistance earns its place by reducing setup while leaving the result understandable and editable.

Describe
Use everyday language to describe a week. Review the proposed structure before saving.
Import
Paste an existing routine. Parsed lifts, sets and reps are validated; starting loads use training rules.
Stay in control
Names and plans remain editable. AI can suggest a name; a local fallback is available.

Screenshot, notebook and other-app image extraction remain future work.

Reliability / the app at the gym

Training stays
with you.

A poor connection should not cost someone their plan or their logged effort.

Offline
After a successful online cache, saved plans, set logging, the timer and local history remain available.
Honest limits
Crew updates, AI services and video need a connection. Written exercise guidance stays available.
Ownership
Export and restore local sessions, notes and photos. Photos stay on the device unless shared.

How I work / design, implement and verify

Make it concrete.
Then check it.

I used the working app, identified friction and directed specific revisions with Claude and Codex.

Design review
Plate entry moved from an unexplained switch to a working demo. Trying it does not log a set.
Interaction checks
Reviews covered contrast, focus handling and touch targets. Bar ends replaced tiny individual plate targets.
Engineering
824 tests across 42 files passed on October 6. A schedule repair now preserves existing days when adding another.

Engineering checks verify implementation; task-based user testing and retention measurement are still needed.

Outcome / what shipped and what comes next

Designed for consistency.
Ready to test the outcome.

Delivered

A working app linking the first workout, editable routines, training history and a supportive return.

Learned

Building exposed details the prototype had not resolved: too much scrolling, unclear saves and effort disappearing from the record.

Next

Test building and reusing a workout, then returning after a gap. Measure task effort and return behavior; retention gains are not yet established.

A future hypothesis

Attach a saved TikTok tip to a lift and surface it next session. Compare time to find the intended reference with the person’s current method.
