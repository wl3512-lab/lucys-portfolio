HabitaBull Lifting
problem statement

Motivation fades
before the habit forms.

Many people begin a workout regimen but lose focus and motivation after a short amount of time.

Conventional gym apps frequently lack elements for goal-setting and habit-building, which are essential for continuous motivation and long-term involvement. In the absence of functional tools for establishing goals and forming habits, users will encounter difficulties in establishing their exercise routines. This undermines the possible advantages of regular exercise due to high drop-off rates and decreased retention.

One question

The purpose was to understand the experiences, motivations and app-specific critiques of people trying to meet physical goals with the workout apps already on the market.

Every interview opened with "Do you or have you used workout apps?" and followed a different branch for yes, I used to, and no, so lapsed users could talk about why they stopped and new users about what held them back.

synthesis / opportunities

From findings
to features.

Each finding became an opportunity, and each opportunity a set of candidate features. The ones that made it into the 2023 prototype: favourite exercises, progress photos compared month to month, animated instruction for every lift, shareable workouts, a written "reason I want to workout" shown back on the interface, and a weekly training calendar.

Held for later: auto-generated routines from preferences and equipment, and suggested exercises based on favourites. Both shipped in 2026.

HabitaBull Lifting
synthesis / findings to build

What each finding
became, three years on.

  1. One thing per screen, made structuralExactly one orange action per screen and never two, because orange is the only fill that isn't a shade of the ground. The tab bar is absent during a workout: mid-session there is one thing to do.
  2. History is the engine, not a feature beside itClear every target rep and the weight goes up one increment; miss on three consecutive sessions and it cuts 10% and rebuilds; anything else repeats. Repeating is a valid outcome.
  3. Logging survives one thumb, mid-set56px targets with 44px as a floor, tabular figures so columns don't jitter, and a keyboard that is opt-in: it appears only after a deliberate tap on the number, never on entering the screen.
HabitaBull Lifting
synthesis / findings to build

And the three that
reshaped the frame.

  1. A crew joined by a code somebody reads outNo account and no password anywhere in the product. What a crew sees is who trained on which day and a photo they chose to share.
  2. Every lift teaches itselfAll 53 carry a form cue, three or four ordered steps and at least two named mistakes, enforced by a test rather than by diligence. Shown once, on the set where it usually goes wrong.
  3. The week is built from her, not from a catalogueGenerated from level, the days she can actually keep and the equipment she has. "Something hurts today" rebuilds the day and changes only today.
HabitaBull Lifting
synthesis / the one that was overruled

Miles wanted a
leaderboard. He
didn't get one.

Finding 04 asked for community, and the loudest form of it in the interviews was ranking. The 2023 flow built exactly that. The 2026 build took it out, because ranked by absolute load a leaderboard shames the beginner this product was written for, and beginners quit ladders: finding 05 arguing against finding 04.

What replaced it came from I Am Sober: a monthly target that is her own schedule counted, and a week of dots per person. Presence, not performance.

There is no weight and no ranking in the crew, and the database has no column for one.

brand / the mascot

Five poses,
one rule each.

He turns up where there is something to react to, and nowhere else. Which pose is decided by what just happened, never by which screen it is. The confused one carries the rule that matters: it is for when the app cannot answer, never for when you got something wrong.

Each pose carries its own aspect ratio rather than sharing one number: standing is 0.84, lying down is 1.13. One shared constant squashed four of them, which is the bug that stretched him the last time the artwork changed.

2026 / what shipped

Most gyms are
a dead spot.

Weight rooms are in basements. Everything the app knows already lived on the phone, but it still could not open without a network, because the page itself had to be fetched.

A service worker caches the shell at install, so one visit at home is enough. The document is still fetched network-first, the rule that stops this becoming the usual installed-app failure of serving a build from three weeks ago.

Underground the plan, logging, the timer and the record all work. Two things need a signal, so two things say so: the crew screen used to report "Just you, for now." to somebody with five people in her crew.

2026 / shipped software

One day added.
The whole week gone.

A tester wanted a cardio day in the middle of a week she had already built. She reported it as “it defaults to leg day and doesn’t save”. Reproducing it showed something worse.

What happened
Adding one day to Push / Pull / Legs rebuilt all four as Full body, and replaced every lift she had chosen.
Why
The plan generator took day types as a list aligned to the sorted days, so which day was which lived in an array index. Insert a Tuesday and everything after it shifts.
The rule now
Editing which days you train does not touch the days you kept. Only a new day is generated.
Then the class
Scanning for the same shape found two more: the Full body A/B variant was also positional, and two days of one type shared a single saved shape.

Nine tests hold it, including the insert-in-the-middle case that failed before. Four more candidates were checked and found safe, which is the half of a scan worth writing down.
