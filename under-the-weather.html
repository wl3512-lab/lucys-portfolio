Under the Weather

A weather app that translates data into feeling. Instead of "Rain: 72°F," it says, "Feels like: At least you have an excuse to cancel plans now."

I built it for people who check the weather to read it as a sign from the universe, the ones who say "It's raining again, of course it is" and take it personally. The idea came from watching New York's collective mood swings: bright April mornings lift a whole subway car, gray October afternoons slow everyone's walk.

Concept

I wanted to find out whether a working app could also carry an idea. Weather data is neutral and people are not, and the app lives in that gap, showing how quickly we read feeling into numbers that have no opinion about us.

Technical approach

The app pulls live NYC weather from the OpenWeatherMap API and maps the conditions to emotional states with if/else logic. Background color and emoji shift with the forecast. A Date object handles the NYC time zone offset, and the app falls back to a clear message when the API call fails.

"Does this app successfully feel unnecessary, like it's solving a problem that didn't need solving?"
User testing
  • One tester pressed the screen instead of the button. Fixed by rewriting the CTA text to make the affordance clearer.
  • A tester suggested making users enter latitude/longitude manually, for humor. Left as a note.
  • Successes: the API call updated in real time; colors and emojis matched conditions accurately on first pass.
Next steps
  • Add a "feels like" line with richer, randomized emotional phrases.
  • Experiment with ambient sound and gradient animation tied to weather moods.
