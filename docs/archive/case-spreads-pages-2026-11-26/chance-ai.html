Chance AI

See the final design ↓

Team lead · AStudio, NYU IMA · with Annie Yu, Jinho (David), Nina and River · Sep to Nov 2026

Chance AI is a live app that answers questions about a photo.

People snap anything, get an answer, or run a visual skill another user made. It is on iOS and Android. Through AStudio at NYU IMA, our team of five redesigned it with the client: the result should stop reading like a chatbot, and more people should use and make visual skills.

01 The audit · September

The result screen read like a ChatGPT reply.

It opened on a long paragraph, the product thumbnail showed the wrong colour and the ask bar looked like a chat composer.

I audited every screen before anyone drew.

I logged each problem on seven screens with a fix. River and Nina studied Google, Apple Visual Intelligence, Pinterest and Bing.

The wireframes fixed the findings in grey first.

Each low-fi screen carries numbered notes back to the audit, so the team agreed on structure before colour.

02 Visual skills · led by Jinho (David)

Visual skills had to feel like a community gallery.

Our question: how might we make it feel like a living gallery rather than a catalog of tools? Week 8 was a list of tools, Week 9 tried a Community feed, Week 10 went back to filters, and Jinho (David) put the creators back on full-photo cards.

Making a skill became five clear steps.

The old flow was a chatbot with fixed example prompts. Jinho (David) designed a linear flow and I helped shape its steps: pick an interest, choose a tool, watch it build, try it, save it.

03 Home · led by me

Home had to promote Snap to know and visual skills equally.

Snap to know is the main feature, but the client also wants more people on visual skills. The original carousel of skills sat on one big Snap to know button. Our question: how might we give both equal weight without a cliché Facetune-style layout?

I gave the carousel its own Try it and put both actions side by side.

Nina refined my first Snap to know layout, and River worked on the proposal with me. Three decisions shaped the final Home:

  1. The carousel shows skills of the day. We kept it because it brings people to skills; a small Try it on each card replaces the one big Snap to know button.
  2. Two equal actions. Snap to know and Create visual skill sit side by side, at the same size.
  3. Recent visual skills replaces Try photos, so Home keeps pointing people back to skills.

04 The final design · November

The team chose Jinho’s frosted bubble style for every screen.

Jinho (David) proposed frosted buttons and soft pastel light behind the screens. Nina carried it into the result and Ask views, which now lead with the product, its price and Exact match.

An audit gives a team something to agree on.

Tie each change to a finding.

Every change in the wireframes carries the number of the audit finding it fixes, so anyone reviewing can see why it changed.

Name the question before the screens.

Writing each page’s How might we gave the team one test for every week’s version.

Next is testing with students.

Most users are college students, so the next round is sessions with them on the new Home and Visual skills.

How it was made

Team

AStudio is a mentorship program at NYU IMA. I led a team of four (Jinho (David), Nina and River, with Annie Yu producing) and met our mentor every two weeks from 25 September.

Process

Audit on 28 September, low-fi the next day, hi-fi from 5 October, brand pass agreed 8 October, Home and Visual skills reworked weekly through weeks 8 to 10, a clickable Figma prototype on 9 October, then the bubble-style proposal on 30 October.

Tools

Figma for every frame and the prototype. I built a hi-fi clickable prototype with Claude to test the camera and analyzing screens, which the app did not have.
