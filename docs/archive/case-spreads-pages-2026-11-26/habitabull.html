HabitaBull Lifting

View the slides PDF ↗

In progress · Designer and Developer · 2023 research, 2026 web version, native iOS app for the App Store

Most people quit within three months.

71–80% of users abandon their fitness app within 3 months of installing it (Dr. Muscle, StriveCloud, 2024). I audited 6 fitness apps, studied sobriety apps, and ran 6 in-person interviews. Every app I audited had the same problems.

The app opens straight to logging a workout.

My first prototype opened with goal-setting and tested badly: people wanted to log a workout first. Today is one button, and the steppers work one-handed between sets, with last time's numbers right there.

The leaderboard is gone.

Ranked by absolute load, it shames the beginner the whole study was written for. A monthly challenge measured against your own schedule replaced it. The 2023 tutorial came back as a form cue: the bull speaks once, on the set where the mistake usually happens.

Comebacks are counted on purpose.

Your answer to “Why do you lift?” comes back on the days you do not feel like it. After a gap, the app says the gap does not undo anything. A crew sees who trained on which day, with no weights and no ranking.

The mascot carries the brand.

The 2026 bull was redrawn with a closed, easy smile, because the app talks to you most on the days you nearly did not come. The Oxide pass matched every colour in chroma to the brand cyan, with one action colour per screen.

The 2023 findings became the build's spec.

Sobriety apps showed me how to keep people coming back.

Studying sobriety apps was an odd move for a fitness project. But I Am Sober had already cracked the retention problem I was working on. Three years on, “why do you lift?” is still the second screen.

The mascot did real UX work.

The bull started as decoration. By the end he gave the app a voice and a reason to celebrate a PR. In the build he also had to survive 48px, which is how a grin became a smile and a bunny became a bull.

The leaderboard problem only showed once real people used the app.

The research said beginners quit when they feel ranked, and the leaderboard survived that finding on paper for three years. It did not survive a week of real sessions. That is the strongest argument I have for designers building the thing.

In progress. I am building HabitaBull as a native iPhone app, headed for the App Store. The web version came first and is its design spec: open the web version ↗

How it was made

Research

I audited Strava, NTC, Sworkit, Freeletics, 7min and RunAway. The 6 interviews, in Vancouver, covered people who formerly used, currently use and never used fitness apps. I tested lo-fi prototypes against four tasks in Figma.

Audits

Three audits, each recording what was measured and what changed. Every control clears 44 by 44, and contrast was computed for all 21 token pairs in use. One knowing deviation is written down: the consistency grid's missed cells stay below 3:1 on purpose, because raising them makes every skipped day as loud as every completed one.
