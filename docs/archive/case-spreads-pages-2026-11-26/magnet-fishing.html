Magnet Fishing

Play on itch.io ↗

Art direction + Build · with Braxton Hauw · 2nd of 20 entries

You pull up a clump of junk and free it one piece at a time.

The magnet pulls up one tangled clump of junk. A piece comes free only when nothing above it overlaps it, so every pile is a small puzzle.

Each river is its own place and time of day.

Rope Strength opens the next river, and each one has its own scene, music and bigger piles.

Every upgrade shows on the tool.

Five tools, three levels each. The magnet grows and turns gold, and the rope goes from old twine to a golden braid.

The bell comes back in three parts.

One part in each river, so the next one stays in sight. Rebuild it and the bell rings again at dawn.

Show progress where the player is already looking.

Upgrades have to be seen.

A number going up in a menu is easy to miss. Drawing the bigger magnet and the new braid in the angler's hand means every throw shows what the coins bought.

A map fix turned the rope into the key between levels.

A stronger rope started as a fix for a canal that overlapped the other rivers. It gave each river its own scene and turned the rope into the key between levels.

Split the goal so the next step is always close.

One bell at the end of three rivers was too far away to pull anyone forward. Three parts, one per river, keep the next one in sight.

How it was made

Stack

Plain JavaScript modules drawing on one 960 x 600 canvas, with no engine and no dependencies. Every number in the game, from item values to upgrade prices, lives in one data file. The music, the river ambience and every sound effect are synthesized at runtime with the Web Audio API; there are no audio files.

AI use

Claude (Anthropic) adapted my artwork into SVG and generated the game code, item text and documentation. OpenAI Codex helped with testing, bug fixes and code improvements. No image-generation model was used, and the game's credits list every piece.

Testing

29 automated tests cover the pile rules, the tutorial clump, the rivers and the bell parts. Browser playtests by AI testers before the deadline caught a sale that paid out about fifteen times over, fixed before submission.
